# Before you launch

Before launching, confirm what the product actually does, who it is for, and what the one action you want a visitor to take is. A solo operator should launch something focused and complete rather than something broad and half-finished, because there is no team to cover the gaps.

## Know the one thing

A launch goes better when the product does one thing clearly than when it does many things vaguely. Before going live, be able to state in one sentence what the product does and who it is for. That clarity drives every other decision, from the page copy to the single action you want visitors to take. A product without a clear one-thing is hard to launch because it is hard to explain.

## Define the one action

Every launch should have one primary action you want a visitor to take: sign up, buy, join the list, start the tool. Knowing that action focuses the whole site on guiding people toward it. A page that asks for many things at once, or nothing clearly, scatters attention. Naming the single most important action before launch is what lets you design everything else to support it.

## Complete beats impressive

A solo operator is tempted to add features and polish while neglecting the unglamorous foundations. The better instinct is to ship something complete and solid in a narrow scope rather than something impressive but fragile. The parts that are easy to skip, legal pages, backups, security, are the ones whose absence causes real problems. Completeness in the basics matters more at launch than breadth of features.

## Order of operations

Work the foundations before the polish. Confirm the legal pages exist, the security basics are in place, and the technical foundations hold, then refine the content and conversion path, then prepare distribution. Doing it in this order means that if you run out of time, what is missing is polish, not protection. A solo operator who polishes first and secures last risks launching something that looks ready but is exposed where it counts.

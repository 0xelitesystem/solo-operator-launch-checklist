# Legal and policies

Before launch, make sure the required legal pages exist and are linked: a privacy policy if you collect any data, terms of service, a refund policy if you sell anything, and the disclosures that apply to your content, such as affiliate or AI disclosure.

## Privacy policy

If your site collects any personal data, even just email addresses, it needs a privacy policy stating what you collect, why, how long you keep it, who you share it with, and how someone can request their data or its deletion. This is both an expectation and, in many jurisdictions, a requirement. A privacy policy is the baseline page for any site that takes in user data, which is nearly all of them.

## Terms of service

Terms of service set the rules for using your product: what users may and may not do, the limits of your liability, and how disputes are handled. For anything that offers a service or sells a product, terms protect you by setting expectations and boundaries in writing. The absence of terms leaves the relationship with users undefined, which is a risk a launch should not carry.

## Refund policy and sales disclosures

If you sell anything, a clear refund policy stated before purchase is both expected and, in places, required, and it should be at least as easy to cancel as it was to sign up. Subscriptions especially need clear disclosure of renewal and an easy cancellation path. Getting the purchase-related policies right protects you from disputes and complies with rules around selling and auto-renewal.

## Disclosures that apply to you

Beyond the standard pages, confirm the disclosures specific to your content. Affiliate links require an affiliate disclosure where the links appear. AI-generated content may require an AI disclosure. Endorsements and testimonials require honest disclosure of material connections. Identify which of these apply to your content and make sure each disclosure is present and findable before launch. Missing a disclosure that applies to you is a common and avoidable launch gap.
